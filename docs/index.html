    About & how to use

    What does the model investigate?

    When every boarding bay is busy, arriving buses form a queue. This model asks how much an extra bay can reduce bus waiting as demand rises. The baseline uses positive weekday morning dwell observations from Warapitiya on Route 654, between Kandy and Digana. Higher demand multipliers are hypothetical stress tests.

    Set up and compare runs

    Choose a preset or set the demand multiplier, bay count, mean service duration, distribution and seed. Start begins a run. Pause freezes simulated time and movement, Resume continues the same run, and Reset clears it. Model and seed changes reset the run.

    Use Visual mode to follow individual buses or Fast simulation to cover more simulated hours. At 60×, one real second advances one simulated minute. You can switch modes and speeds during a run. A teal bar inside an occupied bay shows service progress in simulated time.

    Try the high-demand one-bay preset, then the two-bay preset. Compare waiting and utilisation at similar simulated times. Keeping the seed fixed keeps the arrival sequence the same.

    How to read the measurements

    Queue length excludes buses in service. Mean queue wait is the time from arrival to service start for completed buses. Mean system time adds time in service. Probability of waiting is the fraction of completed buses that waited. The live run starts empty and includes its initial transient.

    Average queue length is the time-weighted number waiting. Bay utilisation is occupied bay-time divided by available bay-time. Throughput is completed buses per simulated hour. These are bus-level measures.

    Assumptions, equations and data

    Arrivals are Poisson at a constant rate λ. A shared first-come, first-served queue feeds identical bays, with no queue limit or abandonment. Service is independent of arrivals. Exponential service has mean m seconds. The lognormal option uses log-scale mean ln(m) − σ²/2 with σ = 0.7759553844290873, giving the same arithmetic mean.

    Offered load is ρ = λ/(cμ), where μ = 3600/m buses per hour per bay and c is the bay count. For exponential service and ρ < 1, Erlang C gives the waiting probability; the mean steady-state queue wait is that probability divided by cμ − λ. GPS dwell time is a service proxy. Real queues can have varying arrival rates, bay interference and road spillback.
